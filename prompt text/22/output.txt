Version 22 — the manholes say how their runs may pass things.

The avoidance rules now live where your Revit family puts them: on the manholes. Each chamber carries the methods allowed to the runs leaving it — around, over, under — read from the instance's obstacle_around / obstacle_over / obstacle_under parameters (your exporter's "params", with a family's "param_defaults" filling in where an instance has none), and editable in a new "Runs may pass obstacles" cluster in the chamber panel. A run obeys both of its chambers: a method is allowed only if both allow it. Those rules are applied to whatever the run meets; an obstacle's own rules can still forbid a method, but say nothing about what the run prefers. A chamber whose runs may not use every method shows it on the plan ("runs pass under only") and in the hover callout.

Around still comes first where it is allowed. Where it is not, an obstacle is no longer a keep-out: the run goes to it and crosses it by the allowed method — and where a run may neither cross nor go round, it fails honestly ("neither crossing it nor going round is allowed") instead of quietly detouring.

A dip that cannot climb back to the entry level in time no longer refuses: the run enters the chamber lower — down to the bottom of the family's conduit window, or just above the chamber base — and its warnings say by how much and why. Where even that is not enough, the run says so: "no room to climb back, and entering MH02 that low would be below its base". In your DCBuild layout that is the case for OBS01 as drawn (0 to −1,500 with the MV clearance needs a dip to −2,201; your manhole base is at −1,100), so the run now reports that rather than going round; a shallower obstacle is crossed under with the entry lowered.

The exporter no longer mistakes a flagged manhole for an obstacle: a family with the a/b/z planes is a manhole and its flags become "params"; anything else carrying the flags is an obstacle.

Verified in a driven browser with your export: the rules land on the manholes, the panel and plan show them, the OBS01 case reports its reason, a shallower obstacle is crossed under with a lower entry shown in the section, the depth in the panel and the 3D view, both-chambers rule, the panel switch, an obstacle's own veto, export and import of the rules, family defaults on a re-export, and a default-drawing case entering at the dip depth — plus the batteries from versions 14 to 21, all passing with no console errors.
