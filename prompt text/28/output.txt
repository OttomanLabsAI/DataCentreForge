Version 28: double-click to connect, the run made on the second face, and undo and redo.

Double-click a face and a connection begins there; double-click a face on another chamber and the run is made and placed on the spot, routed, selected and reported in the status line as connected. There is nothing to approve: the second face is the approval. A single click on the second face completes it just the same, as does a tap on an iPad, where a double-tap begins the connection. Double-clicking the pending face again lets go, a pair that already exists is selected rather than duplicated, and the right-click ring still offers Connect from and Connect here as before.

Undo and redo sit beside the title, Revit's quick-access way, and answer Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z as well, or Cmd on a Mac. Every change to the drawing is a step: a run made or disconnected, a chamber or obstacle added, moved, edited or deleted, a spec changed, a file or Revit export loaded, the example placed, and the whole of a window selection deleted at once. A drag is one step however far it goes, and figures typed into one field in quick succession are one step. The view, the selection and the open windows are left alone, so undo never moves the drawing about, and whatever was selected stays selected if it still exists. The buttons go dark when there is nothing left to undo or redo, and a fresh change after an undo forgets what could have been redone.

Verified in a driven browser with a mouse and with emulated touch: the double-click beginning and completing a run, the single-click completion, the cancel, the duplicate, the double-tap on an iPad, undo and redo of a run, a drag, typed figures, a deleted selection and the placed example, by button and by key, with the view held still and the selection kept, the header still on one row at 1024 px with the new buttons, plus the batteries from versions 14 to 27 including the touch one, all passing with no console errors.
