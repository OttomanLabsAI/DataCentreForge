Version 46: runs sit in the middle of their faces.

Conduits now enter a manhole as centrally as the layout allows. Sliding a run along its face is spent only where it buys something: in full where it lines the run up dead straight, and otherwise only as far as the bends cannot reach. A dogleg of standard fittings can step so far over a given length of run, and the entries now leave the bends that much of the offset and slide away only what is beyond it — so where two faces are well within a dogleg's reach the run takes the middle of both walls and lets the bend carry the whole offset, and where they are further out than that it slides the least it can and keeps the fittings it would have had. Where the offset is too small for even the gentlest dogleg the entries still slide apart to make room for one, as they have since the run stopped tilting.

A face carrying several runs settles the same way. A run that must bend gives way to a neighbour that lines up: the aligned one keeps the slot that makes it straight, and the other comes as near the centre as the pitch allows, so a straight duct is never traded for a centred one and the runs keep their order and their spacing either way. Where every run on a face must bend, they sit symmetrically about the middle of the wall.

On the LV and MV site together — a hundred and eighty manholes and seventy-seven runs — the same sixty-four route and the same thirty-eight run dead straight, the fittings come down from seventy-one bends to sixty-eight, no entry is left against the edge of its wall where nine were before, and the average entry sits twenty-five millimetres off the centre of its face instead of fifty-one. Across a sweep of a hundred and twenty-eight two-chamber geometries nothing lost its route and not one run gained or lost a fitting, while forty of them moved their entries towards the middle. The price is length where a centred run has further to travel — around half a metre on a twelve-metre run.

Verified in a driven browser: faces in line giving a straight run dead centre on both; a little out of line still sliding to line up and staying straight; far out of line taking the middle of both faces and doglegging at 45°; a run more out of line than its bends can reach sliding only as far as that needs; two runs on one face where the aligned one stays straight and the other settles beside it; four runs to chambers off to one side sitting symmetrically about the face centre, in order and at their pitch, with the elevation drawing them about the middle — plus the batteries from versions 14 to 46, all passing with no console errors, and a clean deploy dry run.
