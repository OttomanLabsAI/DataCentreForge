Version 47: the LV model brings its conduits, and runs go under by default.

The LV model now arrives with its own. Its IFC gives 87 banks that join two of its chambers — the Ø114 ducts between the manholes, and one Ø51 — each with the conduits in every row, the side a short row packs to, the level it arrives at and its modelled centreline, so the model can be placed static and keep the route it was drawn on. The 79 chambers the IFC covers now carry their true lid, base and conduit depths and the 210 by 240 centres the model lays its ducts on. The manholes themselves are untouched: still the Revit exporter's, in the same places, with the banks landing on them by Revit element id. Placed on its own it is 105 manholes and 87 banks, 84 of which route; the three that do not say why.

Whether the conduits come at all is now decided at the moment of loading. Both sub-models carry the tick beside their Add button, so either can be placed bare or with its banks, and the whole site the same. Ducts a model brings are also named for it now — LV Ø114, MV Ø128 — rather than all of them being called fibre.

A run passes whatever it meets underneath unless told otherwise: on a new obstacle, on the static manholes and conduit banks a static model contributes, and on a drawing that never said. It earns its place. With the LV model placed static and the MV model routed around it, 145 of the 164 banks find their way where 137 did when everything went around, and those that still cannot name what they could not get under rather than reporting a plan they could not find. The drawing the tool opens with shows it: the two runs now pass straight under the obstacle, marked under OBS01 in plan and dipping in their long section, instead of bending round it.

The array spacing was not working, and now it is. The number lives on the conduit spec, but every face also keeps to its manhole's own lateral spacing, and that floor quietly swallowed anything smaller — setting a spec to 300 against manholes sitting at 450 changed nothing on the drawing, from the spec's window or from the run's. The floor now gives way to the spec wherever a run on it meets a manhole set wider, whichever of the two places you type in, so what is typed is what the conduits are drawn on; nothing is ever widened by it. A run's panel shows the centres its faces are really laid on, and where another, wider run on the same face is holding the grid open, both panels say which.

Two things worth knowing. The whole site is now 164 banks and takes about seventeen seconds to place and route, up from ten; and with both models dynamic on one drawing, 31 of those 164 cannot find a clear path at their level, the two services wanting the same space — placing one of them static, as the Examples window offers, is the way through that. And the picture sent with the spacing report arrives as pixels rather than a file, so the archive holds the words but not the image; the IFC is in it.

Verified in a driven browser: the LV model placed with its checkbox cleared and then ticked, giving 105 manholes alone and then 105 with 87 banks on ducts named for the model, the rows and levels the IFC holds, the true depths on a named chamber, and every bank keeping its modelled centreline; a new obstacle defaulting to under and the demo drawing dipping beneath it, with around still taking it round and an older drawing's own flags read as they were; a static model's manholes and banks crossed under by default and gone round when told; and the spacing typed across and down on a run moving the conduits in the plan, the elevation and the section, with the two panels naming what holds a run wider — plus the batteries from versions 14 to 47, all passing with no console errors, a clean deploy dry run, and screenshots at full width and at 500 px.
